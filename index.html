Privacy policy

Effective and last updated: 4 November 2026

ZiLit ZiLit is a native macOS application maintained by the independent developer identified as unofang on GitHub. This policy explains the current development version of the app and this privacy website.

The app organizes product information, marketing assignments, manual drafts and local review. Optional LinkedIn Page discovery and read-only post/comment viewing require an approved developer application and your authorization. Publishing, broad feed research and AI generation are not available in this version.

1. Data stored on your Mac

The app saves the information you enter: product names, approved facts, audiences, voice, links and asset references; assignment briefs, optional source links and progress; drafts, formats and review status; and activity records with dates. When you configure LinkedIn, it also saves the public Client ID, API configuration, authorization expiry, Page names, identifiers and roles, last verification date, product-to-Page mappings, a user-recorded application review status, and whether content read permissions have been requested.

This workspace is stored as a local JSON file in ~/Library/Application Support/ZiLit ZiLit/workspace.json. It is used to provide the workspace and restore it when you reopen the app. Local content is not automatically uploaded to the developer. There is no developer-operated cloud sync or app account service in the current version. Your own device, backup or synchronization settings may make additional copies.

2. Optional LinkedIn authorization

Connecting is optional. When you choose to connect, the app opens LinkedIn in your default browser. You enter your LinkedIn credentials with LinkedIn, not into ZiLit. The app uses an authorization code, a temporary random state and PKCE verifier to complete authorization through a short-lived listener bound to your Mac's loopback address. These temporary values are held in memory and are not deliberately logged or saved in the workspace.

If authorization succeeds, the access token and expiry are stored in macOS Keychain, configured for access while unlocked on that device. Tokens are not stored in the workspace JSON or sent to the developer. The app does not request a LinkedIn password or client secret.

The app sends the public Client ID, requested permissions and authorization-protocol values to LinkedIn, then sends its access token to LinkedIn's official API to retrieve eligible Page roles and organization names when you connect or refresh. It uses those results to let you map eligible Pages to products. If you enable the corresponding granted read permissions, the app can retrieve published posts from the mapped Page, comment-availability summaries and top-level comment text, actor identifiers and timestamps when you explicitly refresh or load more. It does not provide unrestricted access to the LinkedIn member feed. Retrieved post and comment content is held only in memory for the open content view and cleared when you leave it or change the account or Page mapping. Known authorization-denied responses clear that view. Creating a follow-up brief saves only the source post URL, not the fetched text. This version does not send assignment briefs or drafts to LinkedIn, request posting scopes, or publish posts, create comments, send messages or change Pages. If your developer app only offers the broader administration permission, LinkedIn's consent screen may describe broader access than this build uses.

There is no automatic LinkedIn refresh on app launch. LinkedIn may process authorization, device and request information under its own privacy policy. Availability depends on LinkedIn's approval, your consent and your Page role.

3. Sharing, analytics and AI

The current app contains no advertising or analytics SDK and does not sell personal data or transmit local workspace content to an AI provider. Its optional authenticated network integration is LinkedIn as described above. Opening external links from the app also connects your browser to the selected third-party website. This policy will be updated when material data-handling features change.

4. Retention and your controls

Workspace data remains on your Mac until you change or remove it. The current app has no automatic retention period. To remove the local workspace, quit the app and delete the workspace file identified above. Uninstalling the app alone may leave its Application Support data and Keychain items behind. Manage any copies in your own backups separately.

Use Connections → Disconnect to remove the local LinkedIn credential and all Page mappings. This retains local assignments and drafts and does not revoke LinkedIn's server-side grant. To revoke that grant too, use LinkedIn's permitted-services settings. Reconnecting removes the previous local credential and mappings before starting a new authorization attempt. Expired credentials are not automatically deleted; use Disconnect to remove them. Disconnect before removing the app if you also want its local credential removed.

The developer cannot remotely inspect or erase files on your Mac through this version. You can correct local product information, assignments and drafts within the workspace. For privacy questions or requests concerning information you have voluntarily shared with the maintainer, use the contact route below.

5. Security

The app uses macOS Keychain for LinkedIn tokens, HTTPS for LinkedIn requests, and atomic writes for workspace persistence. Workspace JSON is not separately encrypted by the app; protect your device, account and backups. No storage or transmission method can guarantee absolute security.

Following an external link or contacting the maintainer through GitHub means the relevant service's privacy practices also apply.
